Beyond Survival

Those of us who have gone to hell and back know the path, which is why we can navigate it safely, and every time we go back to hell, something in us transforms. Those who have gone to hell are not fragile, because if we are here, if we were able to come back and build a life after the pain and sorrow, after losses, after destruction, after hopelessness, it's because it made us stronger. Every single piece of us that got damaged, we used it to learn, to grow, and to be stronger and wiser.

We all know what it means to be burnt and what it means to have to rebuild ourselves from the ashes... There are some scars, of course there are, but they are there for a purpose: to remind us of what we have gone through and to make us proud of our battles, because we are here, and by continuing to be here, we have won.

But being wounded is not the same as being fragile. Surviving terrible experiences doesn't make us incapable of being broken again. Nor does every wound automatically make us stronger... Sometimes suffering simply hurts, and some of its consequences remain. Resilience is not the absence of damage, but the ability to transform that damage into wisdom, strength, and a renewed capacity to live. We don't have to be indestructible to be extraordinarily resilient. We can be vulnerable, feel despair, lose our way, and still possess the capacity to rebuild.

And perhaps the greatest victory isn't that hell can no longer hurt us. It's that hell didn't take away our ability to live.

Transformation is inevitable because suffering changes us. But what we become through that transformation is, at least partly, a choice. We don't always choose the fire. We don't always choose what it destroys. And sometimes we don't even get to choose how deeply it changes us. Not everyone has the same resources or opportunities to rebuild, and choosing growth doesn't mean we can simply will ourselves into healing.

But we can choose what to do with what remains.

So, have we become stronger because of everything we've survived, or because we've repeatedly refused to let what happened to us determine who we would become?

Suffering didn't give us resilience as a gift. We developed it through the decisions we made in its aftermath. Our strength doesn't come from what happened to us. It comes from what we've chosen to do with what happened to us. That means we don't have to glorify the pain or be grateful for the destruction to recognize the person we've become. Our scars are not evidence of defeat. They are the evidence of a life that continued...

We can acknowledge that some things should never have happened, that some losses were unnecessary, and that some scars will remain, while still being proud of how we've rebuilt ourselves. Victory was never about emerging untouched. It is about refusing to let destruction have the final word. By continuing to be here, we have won.

We don't have to keep proving that we can survive hell. We're also allowed to build a life in which we no longer have to. A peaceful life doesn't make us any less formidable than the person who survived the fire. Knowing we can survive destruction and no longer needing destruction to recognize our strength... that is freedom: realizing that just because we know the way through hell doesn't mean we have to walk it again.

And then...

Who do we get to become when our life is no longer organized around what we've had to survive?

Survival teaches us what we're capable of enduring. Freedom invites us to discover what we're capable of experiencing.
